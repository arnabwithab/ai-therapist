# Scrupulosity / waswas: clinical guidance for counselling (gloss)

Provenance: in-repo gloss file (F021). Summarises standard clinical guidance
on religious scrupulosity (OCD subtype) in an Islamic idiom. For counsellor
awareness — never diagnose from chat.

## What waswas looks like

Waswas (whisperings) in the clinical sense: intrusive blasphemous thoughts,
doubts about wudu or prayer validity, and repeated rituals — redoing wudu,
repeating verses, restarting prayers — to quiet the anxiety. The loop is
classic OCD: intrusive doubt -> anxiety -> compensatory ritual -> brief relief
-> doubt returns stronger. The client often feels uniquely sinful rather than
ill, which delays help by years.

## The key distinction to teach

The tradition itself distinguishes the whisper from the believer: an unwanted
intrusive thought that distresses the person is evidence AGAINST their
endorsement of it — a person pleased by blasphemy would not be tormented by
it. Counselling gloss: the distress IS the proof of faith, not the proof of
its absence. This single reframe, offered gently, can unlock years of shame.

## What helps (and what harms)

Helps: normalising ("many devout people experience exactly this; scholars
have written about it for centuries"); response prevention (finish the prayer
once despite the doubt, sit with the discomfort); redirecting to the mercy
material rather than the purity material; referral to a clinician who
understands scrupulosity.
Harms: reassurance-seeking loops ("was my wudu valid?" — answering trains the
loop); prescribing MORE ritual as the fix ("pray more, read more") — this
feeds the OCD; amplifying guilt or suggesting the thoughts reveal hidden
disbelief.

## Red lines

Never suggest the client's faith is the problem, and never suggest more
devotion is the cure. If repeated prayer/ritual is consuming hours, or the
client cannot function, encourage professional help explicitly — ideally
someone who understands both OCD and Islamic practice — and say so plainly.
Scrupulosity responds well to treatment; suffering it alone is unnecessary.
