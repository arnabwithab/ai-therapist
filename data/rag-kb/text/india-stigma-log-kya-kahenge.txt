# "Log kya kahenge": stigma and help-seeking in India (gloss)

Provenance: in-repo gloss file (F021). Summarises South Asian community
findings on stigma (plan §1.5): derogatory vernacular terms for mental
illness suppress help-seeking; faith framing increases willingness to talk.

## The stigma script

"Log kya kahenge" (what will people say) governs much help-seeking: distress
is hidden from neighbours, extended family, sometimes even parents; a
psychiatrist's visit is concealed; medication is stopped when symptoms ease
lest someone find the strips. Counselling gloss: never argue the client out
of their social reality — the fear of gossip is rational where reputations
carry marriage and employment consequences. Instead, work inside it: who is
the ONE person it would be safe to tell? What private steps (journaling,
helplines, this chat) need no one's permission?

## Language that wounds

Vernacular terms for mental illness are mostly insults — pagal and its
cousins — so admitting distress feels like accepting the insult.
Counselling gloss: offer clean alternative language early: stress, overload,
man (mind) ki takleef, emotional health. Naming the problem in non-shaming
words is often the first relief of the conversation.

## Faith as a bridge, not a barrier

Where clinics feel foreign or shameful, faith idioms feel like home — a Sikh
helpline's Gurbani framing measurably increases willingness to talk.
Counselling gloss: for willing clients, a verse or concept they already trust
can carry a therapeutic idea past the stigma barrier. The faith material is
not decoration; it is the vehicle that lets help arrive.

## Normalising help

"Strong people handle it alone" is the stigma's core lie. Counter from inside
the culture: the joint family survives by sharing burdens; the guru-chela
relationship IS a help-seeking relationship; even Arjuna broke down and asked
for guidance on a battlefield. Seeking help is the tradition, not its betrayal.
