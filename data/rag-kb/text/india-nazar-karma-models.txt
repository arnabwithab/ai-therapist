# Explanatory models: nazar, karma, and related idioms (gloss)

Provenance: in-repo gloss file (F021). Summarises community findings (plan
§1.5): models like nazar/evil-eye, jinn, and past-life karma are common and
must be explored, not pathologised.

## The rule: explore, never pathologise

When a client says "someone has done nazar on me", "a jinn is troubling us",
or "my karma is punishing me", the clinically correct first move is curiosity,
not correction. These models carry real information — about envy in the
social circle, about guilt, about a family under strain. Counselling gloss:
ask what the model explains for them, who shares it, and what it asks them to
do. Dismissing it dismisses the client; exploring it often reveals the
treatable problem underneath.

## Nazar (evil eye) and envy anxiety

Misfortune attributed to another's envious gaze — sudden illness, a business
collapse, a child's troubles. Counselling gloss: nazar-talk frequently marks
real interpersonal tension: rivalry with relatives, fear after good news,
guilt about success. Address the relationship pattern (who envies whom, what
cannot be said aloud) while respecting the idiom. Protective rituals the
family already uses can coexist with practical steps.

## Karma as self-blame

"My karma is doing this to me" can be a frame for acceptance — or a engine of
self-punishment where every setback becomes deserved. Counselling gloss:
distinguish the two uses. Karma-as-responsibility ("my actions shape my
future") empowers; karma-as-verdict ("I deserve this pain") is the spiritual-
struggle pattern and needs the mercy/compassion response, never amplification.
Note the parallel with the Gita's actual emphasis: action going forward, not
sentence for the past.

## Jinn, spirit, and possession idioms

Distress voiced as affliction — voices, fits, sudden changes — sometimes
reaches faith healers before clinicians. Counselling gloss: coordinate, don't
compete. A healer the family trusts can be an ally in getting clinical care
accepted; the question is always "what helps this person suffer less", asked
with respect for everyone already trying to help.
