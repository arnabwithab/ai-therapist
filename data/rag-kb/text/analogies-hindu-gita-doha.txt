# Hindu analogies for counselling: Gita verses + Kabir dohas (counselling gloss)

Provenance: in-repo gloss file (F021). Verses paraphrased in plain English;
Gita renderings follow widely used public-domain translations (e.g. Arnold 1885).
Use one idea at a time, as an option, never a prescription.

## Karma yoga for outcome anxiety (Gita 2.47)

"You have a right to your actions, but never to the fruits of your actions."
Counselling gloss: much anxiety in students and young professionals comes from
living entirely in the imagined result — the rank, the offer, the approval.
The verse invites a shift of attention from the uncontrollable outcome to the
controllable next step: prepare the application, study the chapter, make the
call. Action without attachment to fruits is an ancient phrasing of a modern
behavioural principle: focus on process goals, not outcome goals.

## Impermanence of feeling (Gita 2.14)

"Contacts with the senses bring heat and cold, pleasure and pain; they come and
go and do not last. Endure them."
Counselling gloss: emotions arrive like weather — intense, then passing. A
client flooded by grief or panic can be invited to notice: this feeling came at
a time, it will also go. Useful for distress tolerance: the feeling is real,
but it is not permanent and not the whole self.

## Lift yourself by yourself (Gita 6.5-6)

"Let a person lift themselves by themselves... for the self alone is the friend
of the self, and the self alone is the enemy of the self."
Counselling gloss: the same mind that drags a person down with self-criticism
can be trained to stand beside them. Pairs well with self-compassion work:
notice when the inner voice becomes the enemy, and practise speaking to
oneself the way one would speak to a struggling friend.

## Equanimity (Gita 2.48)

"Established in yoga, perform your actions, abandoning attachment, remaining
the same in success and failure."
Counselling gloss: steadiness is not indifference. Celebrating a win without
being owned by it, absorbing a setback without collapsing — this is emotional
regulation in verse form. Ask: what would staying steady look like for you
this week?

## Kabir: the slow work of change (doha, public domain)

"Dheere dheere re mana, dheere sab kuch hoye / maali seenche sau ghada,
ritu aaye phal hoye" — Slowly, slowly, O mind, everything happens in its own
time; the gardener may water a hundred pots, but fruit comes only in season.
Counselling gloss: recovery and growth cannot be hurried. A client frustrated
that therapy or effort "isn't working yet" can be reminded of the gardener:
keep watering, trust the season.

## Kabir: where you search (doha, public domain)

"Kasturi kundal base, mrig dhundhe ban maahi" — the musk deer carries the
fragrance in its own navel yet wanders the forest searching for it.
Counselling gloss: for clients chasing reassurance, approval, or happiness
from the outside — the thing sought is often already within reach: their own
values, strengths, and capacity to cope.
