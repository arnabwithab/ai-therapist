# Quran framing for hardship: 94:5-6 and mercy (gloss)

Provenance: in-repo gloss file (F021). Paraphrases of widely used public-domain
Quran translations (e.g. Pickthall 1930). Offer briefly, as an option.

## "With hardship comes ease" (94:5-6)

"So, surely with hardship comes ease. Surely with hardship comes ease."
The repetition is the point: the verse insists twice. Counselling gloss: for a
client convinced their pain is permanent — grief, unemployment, a broken
relationship — this verse is the tradition's own statement of impermanence.
Difficulty and relief travel together; the night does not cancel the morning.
Use it to hold hope without minimising: the hardship is real AND ease is
promised, not instead of the hardship.

## Mercy "more merciful than a mother"

A well-known hadith image: Allah's mercy toward His servants exceeds a
mother's mercy toward her child. Counselling gloss: clients who imagine God
only as judge and punisher — especially those raised on fear-based religious
instruction — often cannot absorb self-compassion because it feels forbidden.
This image gives permission from inside the tradition: if He is more merciful
than a mother, then treating yourself with mercy is not disobedience.

## Dua and istighfar as coping practices

Dua (supplication) voices the need; istighfar (seeking forgiveness) releases
the weight of mistakes. Counselling gloss: both are expressive practices —
naming pain aloud rather than sealing it in. A client who journals, prays, or
repeats istighfar when distressed already has a regulation tool; the work is
to use it deliberately instead of apologising for needing it.

## Night prayer and the quiet hours

Qiyam/tahajjud — prayer in the last part of the night — is traditionally the
hour when supplications are heard. Counselling gloss: many distressed clients
are awake at 3am anyway, alone with spiralling thoughts. Reframing the
sleepless hour — from "something wrong with me" to a time their tradition
honours — can convert rumination time into solace time. (If insomnia is
clinical, treat the insomnia too; framing is not a substitute for sleep care.)
