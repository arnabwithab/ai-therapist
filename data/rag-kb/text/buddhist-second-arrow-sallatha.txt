# The second arrow: Sallatha Sutta for counselling (gloss)

Provenance: in-repo gloss file (F021). Plain-English summary of the Sallatha
Sutta (Samyutta Nikaya 36.6, public-domain translations e.g. Thanissaro,
Nyanaponika). Offer briefly, as an option.

## Pain is the first arrow; suffering is the second

The Buddha's image: being struck by an arrow is pain — unavoidable. Then the
untrained mind shoots itself a second arrow: sorrow, lamentation, "why me?",
rage at the wound — and that second arrow is suffering, which IS avoidable.
Counselling gloss: this is the cleanest separation a client can learn —
between what happened (loss, illness, failure) and what the mind adds
(self-blame, catastrophising, endless replay). Validate the first arrow fully
before touching the second; the client must feel their pain honoured, not
dismissed, before they can lay down the bow.

## Feeling without fuel

The trained mind feels the painful feeling — bodily, fully — but does not
grieve, lament, or beat its breast over it. It does not welcome pleasant
feelings with clinging either. Counselling gloss: mindfulness in one image —
contact without amplification. For anxious clients: notice the sensation,
drop the commentary. For depressed clients: the heaviness is a visitor, not
an identity.

## Using it in session

Ask: "which part of this is the first arrow, and which part is the second?"
Clients usually know instantly. The second arrow then becomes the work plan —
and every reduction in self-attack, every unpicked replay, is progress the
client can feel. The sutta also protects against spiritual bypassing: the
teaching never says the first arrow does not hurt.
