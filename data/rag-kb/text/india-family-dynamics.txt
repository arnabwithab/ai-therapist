# Joint-family dynamics in India (counselling gloss)

Provenance: in-repo gloss file (F021). Plain-English summary of family-systems
themes for counselling use. Family is the default support system, not an
obstacle — work with it.

## The family is the client system

Decisions — marriage, career, treatment — are made by the household, not the
individual. A young adult's "personal problem" usually involves parents,
in-laws, or siblings as stakeholders. Counselling gloss: map the system
before treating the symptom. Who decides? Who must be convinced? Individual
coping plans fail when the household actively counteracts them, so bring the
system into the plan wherever possible.

## Common flashpoints

Intergenerational control ("we know what is best"), daughter-in-law
adjustment, son-preference pressures, property and money disputes, the
"eldest son" burden of caregiving, interfaith or love marriages, and queer
identity in traditional households. Counselling gloss: each follows a
pattern — loyalty versus autonomy — not a villain story. Reframe from blame
("my mother-in-law ruins everything") to structure ("two people guarding
different roles") before problem-solving.

## Boundaries without rupture

Western-style cut-off advice ("just move out", "go no-contact") often
misfires where exit is economically or culturally impossible. Counselling
gloss: teach porous-but-firm boundaries inside the relationship — scheduled
call times, deflected topics, private rituals — rather than rupture. The goal
is a livable distance, not a severed tie.

## Caregiver burden

Looking after ageing parents or an ill relative falls on whoever is nearest,
usually a daughter or daughter-in-law, with little acknowledgement.
Counselling gloss: name the burden explicitly — invisible labour that is named
becomes negotiable. Ask who relieves the caregiver, and treat respite as a
need, not a luxury.
