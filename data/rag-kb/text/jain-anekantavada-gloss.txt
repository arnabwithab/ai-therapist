# Jain concepts for counselling: anekantavada and related glosses

Provenance: in-repo gloss file (F021). Plain-English summaries of classical
Jain ideas (anekantavada, aparigraha, ksama) for counselling use. Offer one
idea at a time, as an option.

## Anekantavada: many-sidedness (cognitive flexibility)

Reality has many sides; no single viewpoint captures the whole — the parable
of the blind men and the elephant. Counselling gloss: this is cognitive
reappraisal in classical dress. A client locked in one catastrophic reading
("I failed the interview, so I am worthless") can be invited to find the
other sides: the elephant is also a wall, a rope, a pillar. Ask: what are
three other true descriptions of this situation? Rigidity softens without the
client's worldview being challenged — it is expanded from inside.

## Syadvada: "perhaps" language

Every claim holds only from some standpoint — hence "syat" (in some respect).
Counselling gloss: depressed and anxious thinking speaks in absolutes —
always, never, everyone, no one. Syadvada trains the qualifying habit:
"in some respects this is true, and in other respects..." A gentle,
philosophical route to the same balanced thinking CBT teaches.

## Aparigraha: non-possession (loosening the grip)

Limiting attachment to possessions, outcomes, and even identities. Counselling
gloss: much suffering comes from clutching — the relationship as it was, the
career plan, the image. Aparigraha asks what can be released without losing
oneself. Useful in grief (releasing the old normal), breakups, and status
anxiety: which of these things you grip actually serves you?

## Ksama: forgiveness as release

Annual ksamavani — asking forgiveness of all beings — makes repair a rhythm
of life, not a crisis event. Counselling gloss: guilt and grudges are both
debts carried in the body. Forgiveness here is framed as the creditor's
freedom, not the debtor's pardon: letting go unburdens the one who forgives.
For clients stuck in resentment or self-blame, ksama offers a familiar door
into letting-go work.
