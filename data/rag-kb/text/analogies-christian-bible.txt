# Christian (Bible) analogies for counselling (gloss)

Provenance: in-repo gloss file (F021). Paraphrases from public-domain
translations (KJV 1611, WEB). Offer one idea at a time, as an option.

## Cast your burden (Psalm 55:22)

"Cast your burden upon the Lord, and he shall sustain you."
Counselling gloss: anxiety is often carried as a private weight the client
believes they must hold alone. The psalm gives an image of deliberate
handover — naming the burden, then setting it down. Useful when introducing
worry-time or thought-defusion: write the worry, hand it over, return to the
day.

## Rest for the weary (Matthew 11:28-30)

"Come to me, all you who labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you
rest... for my yoke is easy and my burden is light."
Counselling gloss: burnout clients — caregivers, exam students, young parents —
hear an invitation, not a command. Rest is permitted, even holy. The verse
can soften the guilt that high-achieving clients feel about resting, and open
a conversation about sustainable pace.

## The lament psalms: permission to grieve

A third of the psalms are laments — raw complaint addressed to God ("How
long, O Lord?"). Counselling gloss: clients taught that faith means constant
positivity often suppress grief until it erupts. The lament pattern — honest
pain, voiced directly, ending in renewed trust — models what therapy does:
speak the unspeakable, then find footing again. Grief needs a witness, and
the tradition provides one.

## The prodigal son for shame (Luke 15)

The son who wasted everything returns expecting punishment and is met with
embrace. Counselling gloss: shame says "you are unwelcome now." The parable
answers from inside the client's own tradition: return is always possible,
and the welcome is larger than the failure. Powerful for clients weighed down
by moral failure, addiction relapse, or family disappointment.

## Gethsemane: acceptance before courage (Matthew 26:39)

"Let this cup pass from me; nevertheless, not as I will, but as thou wilt."
Even Christ asked for the suffering to be taken away — and then accepted it.
Counselling gloss: wanting out of pain is not weakness of faith; it is the
most human prayer there is. Acceptance follows honesty, not denial. For
clients facing unavoidable hardship (illness, loss), this verse dignifies
both the wish to escape and the courage to endure.
